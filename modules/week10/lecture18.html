Lecture 18: Advanced Charts

Small multiples, waterfall, funnel, combo charts, and other advanced forms — used only when they answer a real analytical question better than a simple bar or line.

1. Learning Objectives

  • 1 Build small multiples for category-wise comparison of the same metric.
  • 2 Use waterfall charts for bridge narratives (start → steps → end).
  • 3 Design funnel charts for sequential conversion stages.
  • 4 Apply combo charts carefully; avoid misleading dual axes.
  • 5 Choose advanced forms only when simpler charts are insufficient.

2. Motivation

Advanced charts are tools, not trophies. Use them when the story is inherently multi-step (waterfall), sequential (funnel), or needs aligned comparison across many facets (small multiples).

3. Real-World Story

A finance team explained margin change with a paragraph of text. Replacing it with a waterfall (price, volume, mix, cost) made the board meeting discussion concrete in minutes.

4. Small Multiples

Repeat the same chart structure for each category with a shared scale. Excellent for comparing shapes of trends across regions or products without overplotting.

5. Waterfall & Funnel

Waterfall: bridge from starting value through signed steps to ending value (P&L bridges, variance analysis).

Funnel: ordered stages with drop-off (leads → qualified → won). Keep stage definitions honest.

6. Combo Charts & Dual Axes

Combining bars and lines can work when scales are related and labeled clearly. Dual axes with unrelated units often invent correlations — prefer two charts or indexed scales.

Caution
If you use dual axes, state units, start axes at sensible baselines, and never imply causation from visual alignment alone.

7. Other Useful Forms

  • Bullet charts for KPI vs target
  • Heat matrices for two categorical dimensions
  • Box plots for distribution comparison
  • Slope charts for change between two time points

8. Hands-on Lab

  1. Create small multiples of a monthly trend by region (shared y-scale).
  2. Sketch a waterfall for a simple variance (budget → actual).
  3. Build a 4-stage funnel with conversion rates labeled.
  4. Critique a dual-axis example and propose a safer alternative.

9. Knowledge Check

Q1. Best chart for price/volume/mix bridge to explain profit change:

B. Waterfall
C. Word cloud

10. Summary

  • Small multiples, waterfall, funnel, and careful combos extend the toolkit.
  • Never choose advanced forms for decoration.

Lecture 18: Advanced Charts

When basic bars and lines are not enough — small multiples, waterfall, funnel, combo charts, and careful dual-axis design.

1. Learning Objectives

  • 1 Use small multiples to compare the same measure across segments.
  • 2 Build and interpret waterfall and funnel charts for process and contribution analysis.
  • 3 Combine chart types only when scales and stories remain clear.
  • 4 Decide when dual axes help vs when they mislead.
  • 5 Choose advanced forms only after simpler ones fail the question.

2. Small Multiples

Repeat the same chart structure for each category (region, product, cohort) with a shared scale. Viewers compare patterns, not just totals.

  • Keep axes identical across panels
  • Order panels by a meaningful ranking
  • Great for seasonality by segment without overplotting

3. Waterfall & Funnel

ChartBest forWatch-outs
WaterfallHow a total is built or eroded (start → bridges → end)Too many steps; unclear signs (+/−)
FunnelStage conversion (leads → wins; applicants → hires)Unequal stage definitions; fake “width = volume” if not encoded

4. Combo Charts & Dual Axes

Bars + line can show volume and rate together. Dual axes are risky: different scales can invent correlation.

Dual-axis rules
  • Use only when the relationship is real and explained
  • Label both axes clearly; never hide zero without reason
  • Prefer indexing both series to a common baseline when possible
  • If in doubt, use two aligned charts instead of one dual-axis chart

5. Other Useful Forms

  • Bullet chart — actual vs target vs ranges in compact space
  • Box / violin — distribution beyond mean and total
  • Slope chart — change between two time points across categories
  • Connected scatter — two measures over time with trajectory

6. Practical

For each scenario pick one advanced form and justify:

  1. Revenue bridges from last year to this year (price, volume, mix)
  2. Application → interview → offer → hire rates
  3. Weekly demand pattern for 8 stores without overplotting
  4. Units sold (bars) and margin % (line) by month
Sample picks +
  1. Waterfall
  2. Funnel
  3. Small multiples of line charts
  4. Combo chart — verify dual axis is labeled and necessary

7. Knowledge Check

Q1. Best chart to show how price, volume, and mix changed total revenue?

A. Waterfall
B. Pie
C. Single KPI card only
D. Unsorted scatter

8. Summary

  • Small multiples beat overplotted multi-series charts for segment comparison.
  • Waterfall = contribution bridges; Funnel = stage conversion.
  • Dual axes need discipline — two charts are often clearer.

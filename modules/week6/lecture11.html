Lecture 11: Descriptive Statistics Deep Dive

Central tendency, dispersion, the shape of data (skewness & kurtosis), transformations, and a full statistical summary workflow in Python — drawn from the course EDA and Shape of Data materials.

1. Learning Objectives

  • 1 Compute and interpret mean, median, mode and know when each is appropriate.
  • 2 Quantify dispersion with range, IQR, variance, and standard deviation.
  • 3 Explain skewness (3rd moment) and kurtosis (4th moment) with real examples and decision rules.
  • 4 Choose transformations (log, sqrt, Box-Cox / Yeo-Johnson) for skewed data.
  • 5 Produce a complete describe-style summary and distribution plot in Python (Pandas).

2. Motivation

Mean and variance locate the center and spread of a variable — but two datasets can share the same mean and standard deviation and still look completely different. Higher-order shape (skewness and kurtosis) explains asymmetry and tail risk. Without shape awareness, averages mislead, models break assumptions, and dashboards hide the true story.

From course materials (Shape of Data)
Higher-order moments: 3rd = Skewness (asymmetry); 4th = Kurtosis (tail weight / outlier frequency). Always inspect shape before trusting a single center metric.

3. Central Tendency

MetricDefinitionBest whenSensitive to outliers?
MeanArithmetic average \(\bar{x} = \frac{1}{n}\sum x_i\)Symmetric data; continuous measuresYes — pulled by extremes
Median50th percentile (middle value)Skewed data; robust reportingNo (resistant)
ModeMost frequent valueCategorical / discrete peaksNot in the same way

Order rules (from course slides):

  • Positive (right) skew: Mean > Median > Mode — e.g., household income, casualty counts with rare multi-fatality events.
  • Negative (left) skew: Mean < Median < Mode — e.g., age at retirement, exam scores with a ceiling.
  • Symmetric: Mean ≈ Median ≈ Mode.

4. Dispersion

  • Range = max − min (simple, fragile).
  • IQR = Q3 − Q1 (robust middle 50%).
  • Variance \(s^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum (x_i-\bar{x})^2\) (sample).
  • Standard deviation \(s = \sqrt{s^2}\) (same units as data).
  • Coefficient of variation \(CV = s/\bar{x}\) (compare spread across different scales).

For accident severity analytics, report median casualties with IQR when the distribution is right-skewed; use mean only with clear context.

5. Skewness (3rd Standardized Moment)

Skewness measures asymmetry about the mean. Course formula (Adjusted Fisher–Pearson):

\[ g_1 = \frac{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i-\bar{x})^3}{\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i-\bar{x})^2\right)^{3/2}} \]

Skewness rangeClassificationAction
−0.5 to 0.5Approximately symmetricalMean is usually fine
−1 to −0.5 or 0.5 to 1Moderately skewedPrefer median; consider transform for models
< −1 or > 1Highly skewedTransform or use robust methods; do not rely on mean alone
Why skewness breaks models (course)
Parametric models often assume symmetric errors; extreme right tails dominate squared-error losses; high-density regions get compressed. Solution: transform to stabilize shape before modeling.

6. Kurtosis (4th Moment — Tail Weight)

Kurtosis is primarily about tail weight and outlier frequency, not just “peakedness.”

  • Mesokurtic: excess kurtosis ≈ 0 (like normal) — tails behave as expected.
  • Leptokurtic: excess kurtosis > 0 — heavier tails, more extreme events (critical for risk, accidents, finance).
  • Platykurtic: excess kurtosis < 0 — thinner tails than normal.

In road safety data, high kurtosis on casualties signals that rare multi-casualty crashes occur more often than a normal model would predict — operational planning must account for that tail.

7. Transformation Tactics (Course)

TransformWhenNotes
Log \(y=\log(x)\)Strong right skew, positive valuesCompresses extreme high values
Square root \(y=\sqrt{x}\)Moderate right skew, countsGentler than log
Box-Cox / Yeo-JohnsonNeed optimal powerYeo-Johnson allows zeros/negatives

Always document the transform and invert carefully when reporting original units to stakeholders.

8. Python — Statistical Summary

Dataset: datasets/sample_accidents.csv (course-style accident sample).

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

df = pd.read_csv("../../datasets/sample_accidents.csv")
# Central tendency & dispersion
df[["casualties", "vehicles", "speed_limit"]].describe()

# Explicit skew & excess kurtosis
df[["casualties", "vehicles"]].agg(["mean", "median", "std", "skew", "kurt"])

# Fisher-Pearson skew for casualties
stats.skew(df["casualties"], bias=False)
stats.kurtosis(df["casualties"], bias=False)  # excess kurtosis

# Optional: log1p for right-skewed counts
df["casualties_log"] = np.log1p(df["casualties"])
df[["casualties", "casualties_log"]].agg(["skew", "kurt"])

Expected interpretation: casualties often show positive skew and elevated kurtosis — report median and check tails before averaging for policy dashboards.

12. Summary & Cheat Sheet

  • Center: mean (symmetric), median (skewed), mode (categorical peaks).
  • Spread: IQR and SD; use CV to compare scales.
  • Shape: skewness (asymmetry), kurtosis (tails).
  • Bands: |skew| < 0.5 mild; > 1 severe — transform or go robust.
  • Python: describe, skew, kurt, log1p / Box-Cox.

Lecture 6: Outliers & Noise Handling

Detect outliers with Z-score and IQR, understand their impact on analytics, choose treatment strategies, and practice boxplot-based detection in Python — as specified in the syllabus.

1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • 1 Define outlier vs noise in analytical terms.
  • 2 Apply Z-score and IQR rules to flag candidate outliers.
  • 3 Explain how outliers distort mean, correlation, and models.
  • 4 Choose treat / transform / keep strategies with business context.
  • 5 Build a boxplot-based outlier report in Python.

2. Motivation — Why Outliers Matter

One extreme value can move the mean, inflate variance, flip a correlation sign, or make a KPI look absurd on a dashboard. At the same time, deleting every unusual row can erase the very events policy makers care about — rare fraud, catastrophic failures, multi-casualty crashes.

This lecture teaches you to detect, investigate, and treat outliers deliberately, not automatically.

💡 Key Insight
An outlier is a hypothesis, not a sentence. Ask: error, rare truth, or different population? Then choose a strategy you can defend in the viva.

3. Real-World Story

The “Average” Claim That Hid a Crisis

A hospital reported that average wait time was “stable at 22 minutes.” A single clinic with a coding error had logged several waits as 0. After fixing the zeros and examining the upper tail, P90 wait was over 90 minutes on Mondays. Leadership had been looking at a mean pulled downward by bad zeros and had missed the real operational problem in the right tail.

Outliers and data-entry noise both matter — and boxplots + IQR would have made the right tail visible immediately.

4. Problem Statement

Given a numeric column in an analysis-ready table, how do you:

  • Flag candidates with Z-score and IQR?
  • Visualize them with boxplots?
  • Judge impact on mean, correlation, and KPIs?
  • Decide keep / cap / transform / remove with documentation?

5. Outlier vs Noise

  • Outlier — a point that is unusually far from the bulk of the data (may be error or rare truth).
  • Noise — unstructured variation that obscures signal (measurement jitter, typos, sensor glitches).

Never delete “unusual” rows blindly: a multi-casualty crash is an outlier and a critical signal for safety policy. A typo that turns 12 into 1200 is noise that should be fixed.

6. Detection Methods (Syllabus)

Z-score

Measures how many standard deviations a point is from the mean:

\[ z_i = \frac{x_i - \bar{x}}{s} \]

Common rule: \(|z| > 3\) (sometimes 2.5) flags candidates. Sensitive when the mean and SD are themselves distorted by extremes — circular for heavy-tailed data.

IQR method

Uses quartiles and is more robust to skew:

\[ \text{IQR} = Q3 - Q1 \]

\[ \text{Lower fence} = Q1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}, \quad \text{Upper fence} = Q3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR} \]

Points outside the fences are candidates. Boxplots encode the same fences visually. For extreme tails, some teams use \(3 \times \text{IQR}\) (“far out” points).

7. Impact on Analytics

  • Pulls the mean and inflates variance / SD
  • Can dominate correlation and regression slopes
  • Breaks normality assumptions for parametric tests
  • Dashboard KPIs look “wrong” to domain experts if one bad row survives
  • Clustering and distance-based methods treat extremes as separate “groups”
Quick check
Compare mean vs median and SD vs IQR. A large gap is a red flag that extremes are influencing summary statistics.

8. Treatment Strategies

StrategyWhenCaution
Investigate / correctData entry or unit errorsBest first step always
Winsorize / capExtreme but plausibleDocument thresholds
Transform (log)Right skew + heavy tailReport results in original units later
RemoveProven error; sensitivity analysis OKNever silent deletion
Keep + robust statsTrue rare eventsUse median, IQR, robust models

Order of preference in most projects: investigate → correct → transform/cap → remove only with a written reason.

9. Industry Examples

Healthcare

Lab values of 0 or 999 often mean “missing” or “not done,” not a true measurement. Length-of-stay outliers may be true long-stay patients — keep and report separately.

Banking / Fraud

Extreme transaction amounts are exactly what fraud models need. Cap only for descriptive KPIs; keep raw values for detection models.

Retail / E-commerce

Order value of 1,000,000 may be a bulk B2B order (true) or a decimal error (noise). Segment before treating.

Transport (course data)

High casualty counts are rare and critical. Flag with IQR/Z, investigate, but do not delete multi-casualty events as “outliers” for safety analysis.

10. Python — Boxplot & Rules

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

df = pd.read_csv("../../datasets/sample_accidents.csv")
s = df["casualties"]

# --- IQR fences ---
q1, q3 = s.quantile(0.25), s.quantile(0.75)
iqr = q3 - q1
low, high = q1 - 1.5 * iqr, q3 + 1.5 * iqr
flags_iqr = (s < low) | (s > high)
print("IQR outliers:", flags_iqr.sum())
print("Fences:", low, high)

# --- Z-score ---
z = (s - s.mean()) / s.std(ddof=1)
flags_z = z.abs() > 3
print("Z>3 outliers:", flags_z.sum())

# --- Compare mean vs median (impact check) ---
print("Mean:", s.mean(), "Median:", s.median())

# --- Boxplot (syllabus practical) ---
df.boxplot(column="casualties", by="region")
plt.suptitle("")
plt.title("Casualties by region — boxplot")
plt.show()

# Optional: mark rows
df["casualty_outlier_iqr"] = flags_iqr
Expected habit
Always print counts of flagged rows and inspect a sample before deleting or capping. Screenshot or save the boxplot for your lab report and viva.

14. Flashcards

Z-score formula?
z = (x − mean) / SD · often flag |z| > 3
IQR fences?
Q1 − 1.5×IQR and Q3 + 1.5×IQR
Outlier vs noise?
Outlier = far from bulk (error or rare truth); noise = unstructured variation
Treatment order?
Investigate → correct → transform/cap → remove only with documentation

15. Cheat Sheet

  • Detect: Z-score (|z|>3) and IQR (1.5× fences); visualize with boxplots
  • Prefer IQR when data are skewed or mean/SD are unstable
  • Impact: mean, variance, correlation, KPIs, distance-based methods
  • Treat: investigate → winsorize/cap → log transform → remove only if justified
  • Keep true rare events (fraud, disasters, multi-casualty) for domain analysis

16. Common Mistakes & Best Practices

Avoid
  • Silent deletion of every point beyond 1.5×IQR
  • Using only Z-score on heavily skewed data
  • Treating domain-critical extremes as “errors”
  • Skipping visual checks (boxplot / histogram)
Prefer
  • Always investigate a sample of flagged rows
  • Report mean and median side by side
  • Document thresholds and reasons for any removal
  • Use robust summaries when extremes are real

17. Summary

  • Detect with Z-score and IQR; visualize with boxplots.
  • Impact is real — but rare true events must not be erased silently.
  • Treat with investigate → transform/cap → remove only with documentation.
  • Mean vs median gaps are a simple diagnostic of outlier influence.
